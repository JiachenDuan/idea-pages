What this fixes

A lot of small contractors do not have a sales problem. They have a permit-operations problem after the job is sold. Permit portals, attachment rules, naming quirks, correction notices, and city-by-city paperwork drag jobs out for days or weeks. That delay slows cash flow, creates frustrated customers, and forces the owner or office manager to spend prime hours on back-office admin instead of sales, installs, or crew coordination.

Before
  • Booked jobs sit in limbo because permit forms, load sheets, photos, and PDFs are scattered across texts, email, and job folders
  • Correction notices come back and nobody has a clean list of what is actually missing versus what is already done
  • Owners and office staff waste hours in city portals instead of selling work or keeping crews moving
  • Install dates slip, customers get antsy, and cash collection gets pushed back
After
  • Each job moves through a clear permit queue with required docs, status, and next action visible
  • Submission packets are assembled using repeatable checklists instead of last-minute scrambling
  • Correction notices turn into a clean missing-items list with owner-friendly next steps
  • Booked jobs get to approval and install faster, with less admin chaos

FAQ

How do you handle access and sensitive customer/job data safely?
We work from least-privilege access only, usually a shared drive, exported job packet, or a permit portal seat limited to submission work. We do not need bank access or broad admin rights, and we can work inside an agreed retention window.
What needs to be in place before starting?
You need one supported trade, a small city list, and a repeatable job type like EV chargers, panel upgrades, or HVAC replacements. You also need someone on your side who can answer technical questions or provide missing docs when a correction notice comes back.
What is included versus not included?
Included: packet assembly, submission workflow, status tracking, and correction-response coordination. Not included: engineering design, unsupported jurisdictions, representing technical facts we cannot verify, or broad office-management work outside permits.
How long does onboarding take?
Usually a few days. We review one live job, lock the intake checklist, document the supported submission flow, and start with one permit or one correction round first.
How long do you retain permit materials?
Only as long as needed for the active permit workflow and the agreed reporting window. We can delete working files on request once a job is approved or closed out.
You keep final authority on technical scope, permit accuracy, and customer-facing commitments. We run the permit workflow so booked jobs stop getting trapped in admin delay.
